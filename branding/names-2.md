# Naming Recommendations: Batch 2 (The Heritage & Concrete Register)

These names were generated via a multi-model sweep targeting a 100-year-old advisory firm or premium design studio vibe. They rely on whimsical concrete nouns, agricultural/industrial metaphors, and portmanteaux that secretly encode the theme of "raw material in, curated structure out" or "harnessing raw power."

## 1. The Heritage Advisory Firms
Classic "A & B" naming structures pairing a raw/filtering mechanism with a tool of measurement or structure.

### Baleen & Brass
* **The Secret Engine:** *Baleen* is the bristly filter-feeding system inside a whale's mouth; *brass* is the structural metal of the maritime age.
* **The Pitch:** "We happily swallow your entire ocean of noise, provided we get to keep the few vital truths that remain."

### Quince & Caliper
* **The Secret Engine:** A *quince* is a notoriously hard, astringent fruit that requires intense, slow processing to become a sweet, structured paste; a *caliper* measures exact dimensions.
* **The Pitch:** "We take the most stubbornly unpalatable raw materials and measure them into something exquisitely refined."

### Chinois & Rhumb
* **The Secret Engine:** A *chinois* is an extremely fine conical kitchen sieve used to force lumpy ingredients into perfect purees; a *rhumb* is a precise navigational line on a nautical chart.
* **The Pitch:** "For when the raw ingredients are brutally complex, but the final direction must be impossibly smooth."

## 2. The Concrete Governance Nouns
Single, highly ownable words that sound like premium design studios or heritage manufacturers, but secretly name a physical gating mechanism.

### The Tundish
* **The Secret Engine:** A *tundish* is a broad, heavy funnel used in foundries to safely gate and structure the flow of dangerous, raw molten metal into molds.
* **The Pitch:** "Bring us your white-hot, volatile chaos, and we will calmly pour it into the exact molds of your business."

### Muselet
* **The Secret Engine:** The wire cage that holds the cork on a champagne bottle.
* **The Pitch:** "The pressure inside is immense; we are the small, deliberate structure that prevents an explosion and guarantees a celebration."

### Banneton
* **The Secret Engine:** The wicker proofing basket that gives sourdough its shape.
* **The Pitch:** "Raw expansion is formless and chaotic; we provide the quiet boundary that turns a sprawling mass into a structured whole."

### Whippletree
* **The Secret Engine:** The wooden crossbar that distributes the pull of draft animals.
* **The Pitch:** "Raw horsepower will tear the carriage apart; we balance the load so the engine actually moves you forward."

## 3. The Whimsical Portmanteaux
Single-word coinages that sound like boutique studios, hiding the "raw-in, structure-out" mechanic in soft, agricultural imagery.

### Figwharf
* **The Secret Engine:** A small old wharf where strange cargo arrives loose.
* **The Pitch:** "Where raw, loose abundance arrives to be weighed, sorted, docketed, and sent onward as a proper consignment."

### Pearlatch
* **The Secret Engine:** A pear ripens by nature, but the latch decides when the pantry opens.
* **The Pitch:** "Quiet governance over things that would otherwise spoil or spill."

### Quincewright
* **The Secret Engine:** The quince is useless raw and magnificent once patiently set.
* **The Pitch:** "The firm that turns hard fruit, noise, and pectin into something structured and cuttable."
