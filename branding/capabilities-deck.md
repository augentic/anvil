# Change critical systems without losing what matters

Propellerhead

Evidence-backed modernization for critical, poorly understood systems.

**The promise:** recover what the system must do, consider the consequences with accountable people, and modernize it in bounded, reviewable waves.

The same discipline extends selectively to new long-lived or high-consequence systems.

---

# A primary challenge and a selective extension

## An existing critical system must change

- Its platform, vendor, cost, security posture, or workforce is no longer sustainable.
- The organisation depends on behaviour that is only partly documented.
- Code, tests, documentation, runtime behaviour, and stakeholder accounts disagree.

## For selected clients, a new critical system must be created

- Agent-assisted implementation can begin before intent and consequences are clear.
- Today's infrastructure choice risks becoming embedded throughout application logic.
- The organisation needs speed without allowing generated code to become accidental authority.

In both cases, the organisation needs a defensible boundary for action.

---

# Modernization fails before the new code is written

Most programmes begin with a target architecture, a backlog, and a delivery plan.

The dangerous assumption is that the organisation already knows what the old system must continue to do.

When that assumption is wrong:

- hidden behaviour becomes late scope;
- defects are preserved accidentally while obligations are missed;
- tests confirm the implementation rather than the business outcome;
- acceptance becomes subjective;
- fixed-price risk turns into claims, delay, or quiet quality loss.

Faster code production amplifies this problem. It does not solve it.

---

# New systems also fail before the code is written

The failure is different but related.

- An attractive prototype becomes an architecture without sufficient reflection.
- Product intent is reduced to the prompt that happened to be written.
- People affected by the system enter the conversation after decisions harden.
- Candidate-authored tests confirm the generated answer.
- Cloud and managed-service SDKs make temporary infrastructure choices expensive to reverse.

The question is not whether agents can produce the code. It is whether people have considered what deserves to be built and what the organisation is prepared to own.

# Our proposition

Propellerhead modernizes critical systems deliberately and selectively builds new systems where the consequences justify the method.

We:

1. establish or recover the system's intended behaviour from available evidence;
2. expose contradictions, assumptions, unknowns, and verification gaps;
3. create room for accountable people to consider consequences and resolve material questions;
4. shape delivery into bounded outcomes;
5. implement and verify against reviewed artifacts;
6. preserve the agreed result and infrastructure capability boundary for future change.

The method is designed to keep the delivered result connected to the evidence, decisions, and verification that support it.

---

# What is different from conventional software delivery

Credible firms already perform discovery, architecture, and incremental delivery. Those practices are necessary, but they do not by themselves keep intent, recovered behaviour, human decisions, and infrastructure optionality load-bearing through delivery.

Our distinction must be demonstrated in the continuity of the record:

- source accounts remain distinct and requirements retain provenance;
- conflicts are resolved by named authority rather than workshop consensus disappearing into a backlog;
- unknowns and deferrals remain visible after planning;
- reviewed specifications become exact delivery inputs;
- verification is tied to the delivered candidate and states the assurance actually earned;
- infrastructure capabilities are separated from supported host backends where Omnia is used;
- the agreed result becomes the starting point for the next wave.

Strangler patterns, re-platforming, replacement, and coexistence are implementation choices within that boundary—not the proposition itself.

AI tools may help perform the work. Propellerhead remains accountable for establishing and delivering the outcome.

# Stage 1 — Definition and readiness

## Purpose

Turn “we need to build or replace this critical system” into an evidence-backed decision about what can responsibly happen next.

## Activities

- establish the outcome, affected people, constraints, interfaces, environments, and ownership;
- for existing systems, recover evidence from code, documentation, tests, runtime observations, and people;
- identify critical journeys, contradictions, unknowns, and verification gaps;
- identify required infrastructure capabilities without prematurely binding them to providers;
- assess security, data, model, deployment, and delivery constraints;
- define a bounded first wave and its acceptance conditions.

## Outcome

Proceed, remediate specific gaps first, or stop and reframe—with evidence for the decision.

# Readiness engagement mechanics

The written scope establishes:

- the decision and system boundary;
- evidence and environment access;
- named client participants and decision-makers;
- review dates and acceptance responsibilities;
- approved model, data, residency, and retention conditions;
- outputs, duration, fee, assumptions, and scope-change treatment.

Client participation normally includes an executive sponsor, technical lead, system and operational experts, and relevant security, data, risk, or assurance stakeholders.

If the available model deployment cannot satisfy the agreed handling conditions, the affected material is excluded from model-assisted analysis; if that prevents a responsible result, the engagement is reframed.

The client owns the outputs and may hand them to another delivery partner.

# Stage 2 — Build or modernization in bounded waves

Each wave has:

- a coherent behavioural boundary;
- known evidence and accountable authority;
- visible dependencies, gaps, and deferrals;
- reviewed specifications before implementation;
- agreed verification and acceptance conditions;
- a result that can become the baseline for the next wave.

Senior practitioners and AI-assisted agents implement, test, and review within that boundary.

Where readiness supports a defensible outcome, the wave can be priced around acceptance rather than an open-ended allocation of people.

---

# Stage 3 — Continuous assurance

New build or modernization should not create a system that immediately becomes poorly understood or inseparable from today's infrastructure.

We maintain:

- behavioural specifications and their provenance;
- decision and authority history;
- verification profiles, fixtures, captures, and checks;
- known uncertainty and deferred debt;
- system topology and impact context;
- infrastructure capability and backend decisions;
- the record of accepted change.

Future product, regulatory, and platform changes begin from the agreed baseline instead of reconstructing the estate from memory.

# How delivery works

## Plan

Establish or survey the sources and propose the change topology.

## Review

Named client participants review whether the programme has been divided at the right boundaries.

## Refine

Extract evidence, reconcile sources, expose gaps, and produce specifications and delivery tasks.

## Review again

Named client participants review what will be built and what uncertainty remains.

## Execute

After the service approvals required by the engagement's change-control agreement, bind execution to the exact refined inputs, then build, verify, repair, review, and merge.

## Accept

Publish through the client's controls and preserve the result as the next baseline.

# Evidence and authority remain distinct

Different sources answer different questions.

- Authoritative intent and policy show what a new or existing system should do.
- Runtime behaviour shows what happened.
- Code shows one implementation of the rules.
- Tests show what someone chose to check.
- Documentation shows a stated account.
- Stakeholders and policy establish intended authority.

We preserve source identity and provenance long enough to see where these accounts agree.

When they conflict, authority is explicit. When nobody can resolve the gap, it remains visible rather than becoming an invented requirement.

---

# Human judgment is not delivery friction

As implementation becomes cheaper, the scarce work moves:

- choosing a worthwhile problem;
- understanding who and what the system affects;
- questioning assumptions while decisions remain reversible;
- resolving ambiguity and making responsible trade-offs;
- deciding whether the result is acceptable.

Review points create conditions for practitioners and accountable stakeholders to think well together. They are not ceremonial pauses around the “real work” of producing code.

Agents widen the amount of work that can be attempted. Humans remain responsible for deciding what deserves to exist and what good looks like.

---

# Verification earns a stated level of assurance

Depending on what the system and target currently support, verification can combine:

- builds, static analysis, and target-specific tooling;
- contract, schema, API, and integration checks;
- candidate-authored tests;
- independent engineering review;
- independent fixtures or captures where they can be isolated from the candidate writer;
- operational acceptance evidence.

We distinguish self-consistency from independent assurance. A test written by the delivery candidate is useful, but it does not become independent evidence merely because it passes. Missing independent protection remains a stated gap.

# Omnia preserves infrastructure options

Application logic compiles to WebAssembly components. Omnia runs each invocation in a fresh isolated instance; persistent state remains behind host capabilities.

Application operations declare typed capabilities—configuration, HTTP, messaging, state, identity, SQL, blob or document storage, and related effects. The native host supplies concrete infrastructure backends and retains endpoints, credentials, and deployment configuration.

Where a conforming backend exists, it can be substituted in the host without changing or recompiling guest application logic.

This creates:

- cleaner separation between domain behaviour and infrastructure;
- lightweight local implementations and production backends behind the same capability;
- less application-code churn when a supported provider changes;
- visible capability grants and host-controlled credentials;
- more client choice over infrastructure during the system's life.

---

# Portability is precise, not magical

Omnia does not make data migration automatic or guarantee that services are operationally identical.

A substitution still requires:

- a conforming backend for the capability;
- data movement and consistency planning;
- verification of service semantics and limits;
- performance, resilience, cost, identity, and networking decisions;
- tests against the real target service.

The valuable promise is narrower: a supported infrastructure substitution does not require changing or recompiling guest application logic.

Client feedback identifies this as one of the most significant facets of Propellerhead's offer.

# The role of AI

Open-source models, agents, skills, and evaluation tools will make capable coding assistance available to far more organisations. We welcome that democratisation; access to an agent is not a durable consultancy moat.

We use AI where it expands speed, coverage, or analytical capacity:

- codebase and document research;
- evidence extraction and synthesis;
- implementation and repair;
- test generation;
- specialist review;
- repetitive documentation and analysis.

We do not delegate:

- stakeholder intent;
- authority over material conflicts;
- commercial accountability;
- acceptance of a critical outcome;
- the decision to conceal an unresolved gap.

Agents perform work within the delivery system. They do not govern it.
