# Change critical systems without losing what matters

Propellerhead

We safely modernize critical, poorly understood systems.

**Our promise:** We figure out what your system actually does, agree on what to keep, and modernize it in safe, clear steps.

We apply this same discipline to building new, high-stakes systems.

---

# The two challenges we solve

## 1. An old system must change
- It's too expensive, insecure, or hard to maintain.
- Nobody fully understands how it works anymore.
- The code, the docs, and the people all tell different stories.

## 2. A new system must be built right
- Rushing to write code creates accidental constraints.
- Today's quick cloud choices become tomorrow's legacy traps.
- You need speed, but you also need to own the long-term result.

---

# Why modernization usually fails

Most projects start with a target architecture and a backlog.

They assume the organization already knows what the old system does. When that assumption is wrong:
- Hidden rules become late surprises.
- Old bugs are kept; important features are lost.
- Testing confirms the code works, but not that it solves the business problem.
- Fixed-price contracts turn into delays and arguments.

Writing code faster with AI doesn't solve this. It just makes the mistakes happen faster.

---

# Why new systems fail

- A quick prototype becomes the permanent architecture.
- The real goals are forgotten in the rush to build.
- The people who actually use the system are consulted too late.
- Cloud choices are hard-coded into the application, making them expensive to change later.

The question isn't whether AI can write the code. It's whether we've decided what's actually worth building.

# Our approach

We build and modernize critical systems deliberately.

1. **Discover:** We figure out what the system must do based on real evidence.
2. **Expose:** We highlight contradictions, unknowns, and missing tests.
3. **Decide:** We help your team make clear decisions before we write code.
4. **Deliver:** We build in small, safe, reviewable steps.
5. **Verify:** We test against agreed-upon rules, not just the code itself.
6. **Preserve:** We build it so you aren't locked into one cloud provider forever.

# Our three-stage process

**1. Definition and readiness**
Establish a safe, responsible boundary for the first phase of work.

**2. Build and modernize in bounded waves**
Deliver clear outcomes based on reviewed plans and explicit acceptance criteria.

**3. Continuous assurance and evolution**
Maintain the system, the tests, and the decisions as your business continues to change.

---

# Stage 1: Definition and readiness

**The Goal:** Turn “we need to replace this system” into a clear, evidence-backed plan.

**What we do:**
- Recover evidence from code, docs, tests, and people.
- Identify critical journeys and missing information.
- Assess security, data, and deployment constraints.
- Define the first phase of work and how we'll measure success.

**The Outcome:** A clear decision to proceed, fix gaps first, or rethink the approach.

---

# What readiness leaves behind

- A map of your system and its dependencies.
- An inventory of critical behaviors and the evidence for them.
- A clear record of conflicts, unknowns, and decisions.
- A roadmap broken down into safe, deliverable steps.
- A commercial recommendation for the next stage.

These aren't just slides—they are the actual blueprints for delivery.

---

# Stage 2: Build in bounded waves

Each phase of delivery has:
- A clear boundary.
- Known evidence and accountable decision-makers.
- Visible gaps and dependencies.
- Reviewed specifications before any code is written.
- Agreed-upon tests for acceptance.

Because we've done the readiness work, we can often price these waves based on outcomes, not just hours.

---

# Stage 3: Continuous assurance

A new system shouldn't immediately become tomorrow's legacy problem.

We maintain:
- The specifications and where they came from.
- The history of why decisions were made.
- The tests and verification profiles.
- The map of your infrastructure.

Future changes start from this clear baseline, instead of relying on memory.

---

# How delivery works

1. **Plan:** Survey the sources and propose the change.
2. **Review:** Your team confirms we're solving the right problem.
3. **Refine:** We extract evidence, expose gaps, and write the specs.
4. **Review again:** Your team confirms what will be built.
5. **Execute:** We build, verify, repair, and review the code.
6. **Accept:** We publish the result through your normal controls.

# Human judgment is not friction

As AI makes writing code cheaper, the real value of software engineering changes:
- Choosing the right problem to solve.
- Understanding the consequences of a design.
- Making responsible trade-offs.
- Deciding if the result is actually good enough.

Our review points aren't red tape. They are where the most important work happens. AI does the heavy lifting; humans decide what gets built.

# Omnia preserves your options

We build applications using Omnia. It compiles your business logic into WebAssembly, keeping it completely separate from your infrastructure.

This means you can swap out your database, messaging system, or cloud provider later without rewriting your core application.

It doesn't make migrations magic, but it dramatically reduces the cost and risk of changing your infrastructure in the future.

AI works within our delivery system. It doesn't run it.
